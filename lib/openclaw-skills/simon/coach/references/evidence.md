# Vetted evidence — bounded claims only

Adapted 2026-11-15 from `evidence-vetted.md`, fetched entries only. This is the
vetted list, not a diagnosis guide. Each factual statement gets a short linked
cite to the exact reference below. Explain general signs only when asked;
never apply criteria as a self-test or a child assessment. No medication
content is permitted even if a linked paper covers it.

## Application limits

- The entries are educational boundaries, not permission to diagnose or treat.
- Adult criteria are general; only a clinician evaluates history, impairment,
  multiple settings and alternative explanations. Cite Faraone for peer-reviewed
  diagnostic boundaries; CDC/DSM summaries supplement rather than replace it.
- Child age bands are broad descriptions, not inevitable stages or a checklist.
  Do not describe non-compliance as proof of ADHD. Parent-training evidence is
  not proof that any one suggested tactic works or reduces core symptoms.
- Raymaker is qualitative research in autistic adults, not prospective ADHD
  early-warning validation. Simon's crash signs are his dated reported patterns,
  not a research-validated predictor. Do not generalize a three-month definition
  to his shorter crashes. Work-stress/occupational findings are not coaching scope
  and are omitted here.
- Sleep criteria explain signs, not a finding that he has a disorder. No sleep
  restriction/treatment protocol. Circadian percentages from selected ADHD
  subgroups must not be presented as population prevalence; those claims are
  intentionally omitted. A late night alone proves no sleep problem.
- ACT, self-compassion, pacing and thought-checking studies do not establish
  that an AI coach treats ADHD or prevents crashes. No efficacy percentages
  for Simon, causal mechanism or certainty of benefit. Three items is a
  practical heuristic, not a trial-proven ADHD treatment.
- Any new question or claim outside these limits goes to shared research or
  live web-search MCP and is explicitly **unverified**, with retrieved links.
  Retrieval failure means “no vetted source yet”, not a remembered citation.

### Entry 1.1: DSM-5-TR Diagnostic Criteria for Adult ADHD
- **Claim:** For adults and adolescents aged 17 and older, diagnosis requires at least 5 persistent symptoms of inattention and/or at least 5 symptoms of hyperactivity-impulsivity persisting for at least 6 months inconsistent with developmental level. Symptoms must have presented prior to age 12, manifest across two or more settings (such as home, work, or social environments), cause clear functional impairment, and not be better explained by another mental condition.
- **Short Citation:** (American Psychiatric Association 2022)
- **Full Reference:** American Psychiatric Association. (2022). *Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders* (5th ed., text rev.; DSM-5-TR). American Psychiatric Publishing. https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.books.9780890425787; summarized clinically by Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (2024), *ADHD in Adults*, https://www.cdc.gov/adhd/about/adhd-in-adults.html
- **Type:** criteria
- **Strength:** strong
- **Verification:** fetched

### Entry 1.2: World Federation of ADHD International Consensus on Prevalence and Impact
- **Claim:** An international consensus of 80 experts across 27 countries confirmed 208 empirically supported statements about ADHD based on meta-analyses and large population studies. It established that ADHD affects approximately 2.5% to 2.8% of adults worldwide, is a valid neurodevelopmental disorder with high polygenic heritability, and carries substantial risk for co-occurring emotional, occupational, somatic, and cognitive impairments.
- **Short Citation:** (Faraone et al. 2021)
- **Full Reference:** Faraone, S. V., Banaschewski, T., Coghill, D., et al. (2021). The World Federation of ADHD International Consensus Statement: 208 Evidence-based conclusions about the disorder. *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews*, 128, 789–818. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.01.022
- **Type:** consensus statement
- **Strength:** strong
- **Verification:** fetched

### Entry 1.3: Adult Presentation Trajectory and Emotional Dysregulation
- **Claim:** While overt motor hyperactivity often diminishes from childhood into adulthood, inattentive symptoms typically persist, and adults frequently experience an internal subjective sense of restlessness and executive disorganization. Furthermore, meta-analytic evidence confirms that adults with ADHD exhibit substantially elevated levels of emotional dysregulation and affective lability, which represents a prominent associated feature of the adult presentation.
- **Short Citation:** (Faraone et al. 2021; Beheshti et al. 2020)
- **Full Reference:** Faraone, S. V., et al. (2021). *Neurosci Biobehav Rev*, 128, 789–818. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.01.022; Beheshti, I., Meghna, S., et al. (2020). Emotion dysregulation in adults with ADHD: a meta-analysis. *BMC Psychiatry*, 20(1), 120. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-020-2442-7
- **Type:** meta-analysis / consensus statement
- **Strength:** strong
- **Verification:** fetched

### Entry 1.4: Scope of Practice and What Only a Clinician Can Do
- **Claim:** Only a licensed healthcare professional (such as a psychiatrist, clinical psychologist, or physician) can conduct diagnostic interviews, rule out mimicking medical or psychiatric conditions (such as bipolar disorder, depression, anxiety, or sleep apnea), evaluate clinical impairment. Rating scales alone, neuroimaging, cognitive tests, or non-clinical coaches cannot diagnose ADHD.
- **Short Citation:** (Faraone et al. 2021; American Psychiatric Association 2022)
- **Full Reference:** Faraone, S. V., et al. (2021). Section 5: How is ADHD diagnosed? *Neurosci Biobehav Rev*, 128, 789–818. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.01.022; CDC (2024), https://www.cdc.gov/adhd/about/adhd-in-adults.html
- **Type:** guideline / consensus statement
- **Strength:** strong
- **Verification:** fetched

### Entry 2.1: Developmental Manifestation Across Childhood and Adolescence
- **Claim:** ADHD presentation shifts across development: preschool children (ages 4–5) predominantly display extreme motor hyperactivity and behavioral non-compliance; school-age children (ages 6–11) show prominent academic inattention, disorganization, and peer difficulties; and adolescents (ages 12–18) experience declining overt hyperactivity alongside persistent inattention, time-management failures, and increased risk for internalizing conditions.
- **Short Citation:** (Wolraich et al. 2019)
- **Full Reference:** Wolraich, M. L., Hagan, J. F., Allan, C., et al. (2019). Clinical Practice Guideline for the Diagnosis, Evaluation, and Treatment of Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder in Children and Adolescents. *Pediatrics*, 144(4), e20192528. https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2019-2528
- **Type:** guideline
- **Strength:** strong
- **Verification:** fetched

### Entry 2.2: AAP 2019 Clinical Practice Guideline on Parent Training
- **Claim:** The American Academy of Pediatrics strongly recommends evidence-based Parent Training in Behavior Management (PTBM) and/or behavioral classroom interventions as the first-line treatment for preschool children aged 4 to their 6th birthday with ADHD (Grade A: strong recommendation).
- **Short Citation:** (Wolraich et al. 2019)
- **Full Reference:** Wolraich, M. L., et al. (2019). Clinical Practice Guideline for the Diagnosis, Evaluation, and Treatment of Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder in Children and Adolescents. *Pediatrics*, 144(4), e20192528. https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2019-2528
- **Type:** guideline
- **Strength:** strong
- **Verification:** fetched

### Entry 2.3: Evidence Base for Behavioural Parent Training (BPT)
- **Claim:** A meta-analysis of 32 randomized controlled trials demonstrated that behavioral parent interventions yield significant, blinded improvements in positive parenting practices (SMD 0.63), reductions in negative parenting (SMD 0.43), and reductions in child conduct problems (SMD 0.31). However, when evaluated by probably blinded observers, behavioral parent training showed no significant effect on reducing core ADHD symptoms (SMD 0.02), establishing that its proven efficacy lies in family functioning and behavioral adaptation rather than core symptom eradication.
- **Short Citation:** (Daley et al. 2014)
- **Full Reference:** Daley, D., van der Oord, S., Ferrin, M., et al. (2014). Behavioral interventions in attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder: a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials across multiple outcome domains. *Journal of the American Academy of Child & Adolescent Psychiatry*, 53(8), 835–847. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2014.05.013
- **Type:** meta-analysis
- **Strength:** strong
- **Verification:** fetched

### Entry 3.1: Defining Autistic Burnout and Its Core Triad
- **Claim:** Autistic burnout is conceptualized as a debilitating condition resulting from chronic life stress and a prolonged mismatch between external expectations and internal abilities without adequate supports. It is defined by a primary triad of pervasive exhaustion (typically lasting 3 or more months), loss of previously attained executive and daily living skills, and sharply reduced tolerance to sensory and social stimuli.
- **Short Citation:** (Raymaker et al. 2020)
- **Full Reference:** Raymaker, D. M., Teo, A. R., Steckler, N. A., et al. (2020). "Having All of Your Internal Resources Exhausted Beyond Measure and Being Left with No Clean-Up Crew": Defining Autistic Burnout. *Autism in Adulthood*, 2(2), 132–143. https://doi.org/10.1089/aut.2019.0079
- **Type:** review / qualitative study (CBPR)
- **Strength:** moderate
- **Verification:** fetched

### Entry 3.2: Boundaries and Generalizability of Autistic Burnout to ADHD-Only Adults
- **Claim:** Empirical research on autistic burnout has sampled autistic adults, and the construct is not formally established or validated as a distinct diagnosis for adults with ADHD alone. While underlying mechanisms like chronic masking, executive compensation, and sensory overload conceptually align with ADHD crash cycles, treating it as an established clinical diagnosis in non-autistic ADHD adults is an unverified extrapolation.
- **Short Citation:** (Raymaker et al. 2020)
- **Full Reference:** Raymaker, D. M., et al. (2020). *Autism in Adulthood*, 2(2), 132–143. https://doi.org/10.1089/aut.2019.0079
- **Type:** review / qualitative study
- **Strength:** moderate
- **Verification:** fetched

### Entry 4.1: Diagnostic Criteria for Chronic Insomnia Disorder (ICSD-3 / DSM-5-TR)
- **Claim:** Chronic insomnia disorder is characterized by persistent difficulty initiating sleep, difficulty maintaining sleep, or waking earlier than desired despite adequate opportunity and circumstances for sleep. The disturbance must occur at least 3 nights per week, persist for at least 3 months, and result in clinically significant daytime impairment (e.g., fatigue, cognitive problems, mood disturbance, or reduced work performance).
- **Short Citation:** (American Academy of Sleep Medicine 2014; VA/DoD 2025)
- **Full Reference:** American Academy of Sleep Medicine. (2014). *The International Classification of Sleep Disorders* (3rd ed.; ICSD-3). Darien, IL; Department of Veterans Affairs & Department of Defense. (2025). *VA/DoD Clinical Practice Guideline for the Management of Chronic Insomnia Disorder and Obstructive Sleep Apnea*. https://www.healthquality.va.gov/guidelines/CD/insomnia/
- **Type:** criteria / guideline
- **Strength:** strong
- **Verification:** fetched

### Entry 4.2: Delayed Sleep-Wake Phase Disorder (DSWPD)
- **Claim:** DSWPD is a circadian rhythm disorder defined by a persistent, significant delay in the major sleep period (typically by 2 or more hours) relative to conventional social schedules, causing recurrent inability to fall asleep and wake at socially required times. When individuals with DSWPD are permitted to maintain their naturally delayed sleep-wake schedule without external social demands, sleep quality and duration are normal.
- **Short Citation:** (American Academy of Sleep Medicine 2014; Bijlenga et al. 2019)
- **Full Reference:** American Academy of Sleep Medicine. (2014). *ICSD-3*. Darien, IL; Bijlenga, D., et al. (2019). *ADHD Attention Deficit and Hyperactivity Disorders*, 11(1), 5–19. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12402-018-0271-z
- **Type:** criteria / review
- **Strength:** strong
- **Verification:** fetched

### Entry 5.1: Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT)
- **Claim:** ACT demonstrates strong transdiagnostic efficacy across 20 meta-analyses for anxiety, depression, and chronic pain (Gloster et al. 2020), but ADHD-specific evidence remains preliminary, consisting of small pilot, open-label, and quasi-experimental studies without powered randomized controlled trials confirming core symptom reductions (Munawar et al. 2021).
- **Short Citation:** (Gloster et al. 2020; Munawar et al. 2021)
- **Full Reference:** Gloster, A. T., Walder, N., Levin, M. E., et al. (2020). The empirical status of acceptance and commitment therapy: A review of meta-analyses. *Journal of Contextual Behavioral Science*, 18, 181–192. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcbs.2020.09.009; Munawar, K., Choudhry, F. R., Lee, S. H., et al. (2021). Acceptance and commitment therapy for individuals having attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD): A scoping review. *Heliyon*, 7(8), e07842. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2021.e07842
- **Type:** meta-analysis / review
- **Strength:** strong transdiagnostically; weak/preliminary in adult ADHD
- **Verification:** fetched

### Entry 5.2: Self-Compassion Interventions
- **Claim:** A meta-analysis of 27 randomized controlled trials confirmed that self-compassion interventions produce moderate-to-large improvements in self-compassion (g = 0.75), stress reduction (g = 0.67), depression (g = 0.66), and anxiety (g = 0.57), alongside marked reductions in rumination (g = 1.37) and self-criticism (g = 0.56).
- **Short Citation:** (Ferrari et al. 2019)
- **Full Reference:** Ferrari, M., Hunt, C., Harrysunker, A., et al. (2019). Self-Compassion Interventions and Psychosocial Outcomes: a Meta-Analysis of RCTs. *Mindfulness*, 10, 1455–1473. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12671-019-01134-6
- **Type:** meta-analysis
- **Strength:** strong
- **Verification:** fetched

### Entry 5.3: Activity Pacing and Energy Management
- **Claim:** Activity pacing is recommended in national clinical guidelines (NICE NG206) to prevent post-exertional symptom worsening in ME/CFS, but empirical trial evidence remains weak and contested with high heterogeneity, and there are zero completed randomized controlled trials evaluating pacing in adult ADHD.
- **Short Citation:** (NICE 2021)
- **Full Reference:** National Institute for Health and Care Excellence. (2021). *Myalgic encephalomyelitis (or encephalopathy)/chronic fatigue syndrome: diagnosis and management* (NICE Guideline NG206). https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng206/chapter/recommendations
- **Type:** guideline
- **Strength:** weak/contested empirically; consensus-based guideline recommendation
- **Verification:** fetched

### Entry 5.4: Implementation Intentions ("If–Then" Planning) and Prioritization
- **Claim:** Theoretical experimental frameworks and extensive meta-analyses demonstrate that implementation intentions ("if–then" planning) significantly enhance goal attainment by automating situational cue recognition, although specific heuristics like "limiting to three items" derive from working memory capacity principles rather than standalone ADHD clinical trials.
- **Short Citation:** (Gollwitzer 1999)
- **Full Reference:** Gollwitzer, P. M. (1999). Implementation intentions: Strong effects of simple plans. *American Psychologist*, 54(7), 493–503. https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.54.7.493
- **Type:** review / theoretical framework
- **Strength:** strong for implementation intentions generally; moderate/inferred for brief daily list-capping heuristics
- **Verification:** fetched

### Entry 5.5: Light Cognitive Restructuring
- **Claim:** A network meta-analysis of 45 randomized controlled trials established that cognitive restructuring is effective for reducing depression severity compared to waitlist and care-as-usual, but it shows no superiority over behavioral activation alone, indicating that brief cognitive reframing is an active, useful tool whose clinical benefit is comparable to behavioral strategies.
- **Short Citation:** (Ciharova et al. 2021)
- **Full Reference:** Ciharova, M., Furukawa, T. A., Efthimiou, O., et al. (2021). Cognitive Restructuring, Behavioral Activation and Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy in the Treatment of Adult Depression: A Network Meta-Analysis. *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology*, 89(6), 563–574. https://doi.org/10.1037/ccp0000654
- **Type:** meta-analysis
- **Strength:** moderate
- **Verification:** fetched

## Do not claim


The following popular concepts frequently appear on internet forums, social media, and commercial coaching blogs, but **cannot be claimed as established scientific facts** based on peer-reviewed guidelines and meta-analyses:

1. **"ADHD Burnout is a formal medical or psychiatric diagnosis":**  
   *Correction:* Neither the DSM-5-TR nor the ICD-11 recognizes "ADHD burnout." Empirical clinical burnout research is restricted to autistic burnout (Raymaker et al. 2020) and occupational burnout (Maslach et al. 2001).
2. **"Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD) has documented prevalence figures (e.g., 98% or 99%) in ADHD":**  
   *Correction:* RSD is not an approved diagnostic entity or subtype in DSM-5-TR or ICD-11. Cited 98%–99% figures originate from informal online polls and magazine articles by William Dodson, with zero peer-reviewed epidemiological validation. Rejection sensitivity exists as a general psychological construct (Downey & Feldman 1996), and emotional dysregulation is common in ADHD (Beheshti et al. 2020), but "RSD" as a distinct genetic syndrome lacks empirical validity.
3. **"ADHD individuals have an 'Interest-Based Nervous System' (IBNS) with a unique non-dopaminergic biology":**  
   *Correction:* IBNS is an informal descriptive clinical metaphor created by William Dodson. There are zero peer-reviewed neurobiological or imaging studies validating IBNS as a distinct physiological nervous system.
4. **"Behavioral Parent Training (BPT) cures or reduces core childhood ADHD symptoms on blinded evaluations":**  
   *Correction:* Blinded meta-analyses (Daley et al. 2014) demonstrate that while BPT significantly improves parenting skills and decreases child conduct problems, its blinded effect on core ADHD symptoms (inattention and hyperactivity) is non-significant (SMD 0.02).
5. **"Sleep hygiene education alone is sufficient to resolve chronic insomnia in ADHD":**  
   *Correction:* Both the AASM (Edinger et al. 2021) and ACP (Qaseem et al. 2016) clinical guidelines explicitly recommend against standalone sleep hygiene, establishing that multi-component CBT-I is required.
6. **"Activity pacing has been proven to treat or prevent ADHD burnout in clinical trials":**  
   *Correction:* Pacing is an energy-management strategy adopted from ME/CFS (NICE NG206) and autistic lived experience. There are zero published RCTs evaluating activity pacing in adult ADHD.
7. **"Coaches, apps, or AI systems can assess or diagnose ADHD":**  
   *Correction:* The World Federation of ADHD (Faraone et al. 2021) and DSM-5-TR explicitly mandate that ADHD can only be diagnosed by a licensed clinician through comprehensive clinical evaluation ruling out competing somatic and psychiatric conditions.
